Sector rotation

Generated 2026-11-14. Ken French data library (US industries, 1926–2026), SPDR sector ETFs (Yahoo Finance, 1999–2026). Research only, not investment advice.

Industry momentum held up out of sample: holding the 3 industries with the best past year beat holding all 12 by +3.3% a year from 1990 (alpha +3.8%, t 3.1), using a rule chosen only on 1927–1989. It has been weaker since 1999 (+2.6% a year, t 1.6), and the same rule on the sector ETFs you can actually buy added +0.9% a year since 1999 (t 0.7), which could easily be luck. Short-term reversal and low volatility did not beat holding every sector.

How the test works

  • At the end of each month, rank the sectors by a signal using only data up to that month, and hold the top ones in equal weight the next month. Trading costs 0.10% of the amount bought or sold.
  • Rotation has to beat the simple alternative: holding every sector in equal weight. "Alpha" is the yearly return left after allowing for how much the rotation moves with that benchmark. t above about 2 is unlikely to be luck.
  • Each signal's settings (look-back, skipping the latest month, how many sectors to hold) are chosen on 1927–1989 only. The chosen rule is then tested unchanged on 1990–2026 and on the sector ETFs. Holm-adjusted p-values allow for testing three signals.
  • Signals: momentum (best past return), reversal (last month's losers), low volatility (calmest sectors).

Step 2: business-cycle signals

Moving into defensive sectors when the economy looks to be slowing did not work: no version beat holding all industries by a meaningful margin from 1990 (best +0.4% a year, worst -1.3%) or on the ETFs. Adding the switch to momentum lowered its worst drop but also its return. A regression forecast on the four indicators beat all industries by +3.7% a year from 1990 (t 2.8), but only +2.2% (t 1.8) before 2020, and nothing on the ETFs since 2009 (-0.2%). Worth watching, not proven.

  • Indicators from FRED, each used only from the month it was published: the yield curve (10-year minus 3-month Treasury), inflation, industrial production and unemployment. Warnings: inverted curve, rising unemployment (the Sahm rule), falling production, or any of them. A warning was on 37% of months since 1955.
  • Strategies: hold defensive sectors (staples, health care, utilities, telecom) while a warning is on and cyclical ones otherwise; step 1's momentum but only among defensives during a warning; and a regression forecast of each sector's next-month return from the four indicators, refitted each month on past data only.

Step 3: insider and Congress buying by sector

Insider buying (insiders net buying, 3m, top 2, chosen on 2006–2015) trailed all industries by 1.8% a year afterwards (alpha t -0.4, Holm p 1.00): could easily be luck. Congress buying (congress net buying, 3m, top 2, chosen on 2014–2019) trailed all industries by 6.2% a year afterwards (alpha t -0.6, Holm p 1.00): could easily be luck.

  • Insiders: every open-market purchase and sale by officers and directors on Form 4 since 2006 (808,238 insider-company-month records), from SEC's bulk data sets. Rules chosen on 2006–2015, tested on 2016–2026.
  • Congress: House members' disclosed buys and sells since 2014. Rules chosen on 2014–2019, tested on 2020–2026. Far fewer trades, so a much noisier signal.
  • Each company is placed in one of the 12 industries by its SEC industry code. Signal: (people buying − people selling) ÷ (buying + selling) over the past 1–12 months, by filing date, as is or relative to the industry's own past 3 years. Hold the top 2–4 industries for a month, 0.10% trading cost.

Halal use

The sector ETFs themselves don't pass a Shariah screen: the financials fund holds banks and the others include companies over the debt limits. If a rotation signal were used live, it would mean holding halal-screened stocks inside the chosen sectors (financials left out), not the ETFs.

Limits

  • The industry portfolios can't be bought directly; the ETFs can, but only since 1999 and with different sector definitions.
  • Momentum has had sharp losing spells (the worst 12 months trailed all industries by 33%, in 1933).
  • Costs are a flat 0.10%; taxes are ignored.
