Copying Congress

Generated 2026-11-13. House periodic transaction reports (STOCK Act), Yahoo Finance prices. Research only, not investment advice.

Copying the members with the best past record did not work: they did no better than copying everyone, and no group beat the market by more than luck would explain. Main setup, alpha a year vs QQQ: top members +1.2% (t 0.4), every member +1.1% (t 0.3), bottom members +3.1% (t 0.9). The top group beat the all-members group in 5 of 10 setups.

How the test works

  • Each year, rank House members by how their disclosed stock buys did against SPY over the previous 2 years, using only trades whose 126-trading-day hold had finished before the year began. Copy the top 30 (at least 5 past trades each).
  • Buy each of their purchases at the close of the first trading day after the report was filed, and hold for 126 trading days (about six months). Call-option buys are copied as the stock.
  • Daily returns of the resulting portfolio are compared with SPY and with QQQ. Alpha is the yearly return left after allowing for how much the portfolio moves with the benchmark (beta). t above about 2 is unlikely to be luck.
  • "Every member" and "bottom members" are the same rule applied to everyone and to the worst-ranked, as checks.

Limits

  • Only House reports filed electronically are read. Paper reports are scanned images (most reports before 2018, about 13% now), so members who file on paper, including some heavy traders, are partly or fully missing. The Senate isn't included yet.
  • Stocks no longer listed have no free price history and are dropped, which flatters every group a little.
  • Amounts are ranges, so every trade gets equal weight; members' real positions differ a lot in size.
  • The halal variant keeps shares in permissible industries by SEC industry code. It does not apply the debt and cash ratio screen at the time of each trade.
